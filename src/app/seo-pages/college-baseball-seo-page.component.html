How the College Baseball Model Works

College baseball has the widest talent spread across its bracket field, which is why the model hits 76% on the moneyline — the highest of any sport tracked on Jack's Models. Regional powerhouses like Arkansas, LSU, and Vanderbilt are modeled very differently from smaller conference programs.

The model evaluates each team's offensive production (runs scored per game, batting average, slugging percentage) against the opposing pitcher's ERA and WHIP. Home park context is applied to adjust for stadiums that play significantly differently for run-scoring.

Because college baseball seasons are long and teams play conference series on back-to-back days, the model uses rolling averages that capture recent form rather than just season-long stats.

When Is College Baseball Season?

The NCAA Baseball season runs February through June, culminating in the College World Series in Omaha. The model is active throughout the regular season, conference tournaments, and the NCAA Regional, Super Regional, and CWS rounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is college baseball the most accurate sport on the site?

College baseball has larger gaps in team quality than most sports, especially during the NCAA Tournament when top national seeds play smaller conference teams in regional rounds. The model correctly identifies heavy favorites more often than in even-field sports like the NFL.

How many college baseball teams are supported?

The model covers all major Division I programs. Select any two teams from the scoreboard's College Baseball dropdown to run a prediction.

Does the model account for the starting pitcher?

Yes — pitcher ERA and WHIP are primary inputs. Weekend ace vs. mid-week starter differences are reflected in the team's pitching stats.
