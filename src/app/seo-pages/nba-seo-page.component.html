How the NBA Model Works

NBA scoring is driven by pace (possessions per 48 minutes) and efficiency (points per possession). The model computes an offensive rating for each team — how many points they score per 100 possessions — and compares it against the opponent's defensive rating, how many points they allow per 100 possessions.

The projected score is derived from the expected number of possessions in the game (a function of both teams' pace) multiplied by the blended offensive/defensive efficiency. This gives a points total for each team that accounts for both how fast and how well they play.

When Is NBA Season?

The NBA regular season runs October through April, followed by the playoffs in May and June. The model covers all 30 teams throughout the regular season and postseason. Early-season predictions blend current stats with prior-year data until teams have played enough games for reliable ratings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the model account for rest days?

Rest advantage is not explicitly modeled, but team efficiency stats reflect each team's rolling performance, which correlates with scheduling load.

Can I predict playoff series outcomes?

The model predicts individual game scores. You can run the same matchup multiple times to simulate a series and track projected winners.

How accurate are NBA totals (over/under)?

The model hits 53% on over/under predictions across 400 tracked games, slightly above the break-even threshold.
