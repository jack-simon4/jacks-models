How the NCAAF Model Works

College football has enormous variance in team quality — the gap between a Power Four contender and a Group of Five bottom-dweller is far wider than in the NFL. The model handles this by rating each team's offense and defense relative to the national average (oRating and dRating), so mismatches are priced in automatically.

The core efficiency metric is yards per play, weighted by strength of schedule (SOS) so teams that put up big numbers against weak competition aren't overvalued. A recent-game weighting blends the season average with the last three games (65% season / 35% recent) to capture in-season trends like a team hitting its stride or dealing with injuries.

Scoring efficiency — yards per point — adjusts for teams that move the ball well but stall in the red zone versus teams that punch it in efficiently. Plays per game adjusts for tempo, since some teams run 85+ plays and others grind out 60-play games.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is college football moneyline accuracy so high at 70%?

FBS has extreme talent gaps. Predicting that Alabama beats a mid-major is easier than predicting NFL outcomes where teams are more evenly matched. The model's biggest edge is correctly sizing those mismatches rather than picking 50/50 games.

Does the model work for bowl games and the CFP?

Yes — select any two FBS teams in the scoreboard. The model uses season-long stats so it performs well in bowl matchups even when teams haven't played each other.

How does the model handle early-season games?

In Weeks 1–8, the model blends current-season stats with last year's data to avoid over-weighting a small sample. By Week 8–9, current stats take full precedence.
