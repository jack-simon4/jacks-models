How the NCAAM Model Works

College basketball has even wider talent gaps than the NBA, which is why the model achieves its highest moneyline accuracy (73%) in this sport. The model rates each team's offensive and defensive efficiency relative to the Division I average, so games between ranked programs and mid-major opponents are correctly priced.

Pace is a major factor in college basketball scoring. Tempo-adjusted stats prevent teams that score a lot simply because they play fast from being rated higher than efficient, slower-paced teams. The model projects the number of possessions in the game and applies each team's per-possession efficiency to get to a final score.

The model also incorporates each team's strength of schedule so that an undefeated record against weak competition is weighted less heavily than the same record against major-conference opponents.

March Madness Predictions

The NCAA Tournament is the most popular bracket event in sports. The model covers all 68 tournament teams and can project any first-round, Sweet 16, Elite Eight, Final Four, or championship game. Because the model is built on season-long efficiency data rather than seeding alone, it often identifies mismatches the bracket lines miss.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is NCAAM moneyline accuracy the highest at 73%?

College basketball has the most extreme talent spread of any sport on the site. A No. 1 seed vs. a No. 16 seed is a near-certain outcome. The model's strength is correctly identifying large talent gaps, which are more common in college than pro sports.

Does the model cover women's basketball (NCAAW)?

Currently the model covers the men's Division I tournament and regular season. NCAAW support is planned for a future update.

How many teams are in the model?

The model covers all major Division I programs. Select any two teams from the scoreboard dropdown to run an instant prediction.
